## 1. The warehouse

**Budget: about 25 minutes** if they take the lock, the trap, and the fancy crate. The three clues are all on the office desk. The crate is optional loot, not a required clue. If they poke bulk cargo, it is heavy and worthless tonight — point them at the one crate that isn't, or at the stairs.

Korran is five feet inside. **Power is off.** Metal is grinding **about 15 feet around a wall**, getting louder, reaching up toward the ceiling. Lucy or Pip looks, unless someone hits the switches first. Korran still sees nothing until the lights are on.

**Light switches:** by the back door. They work. Overheads come on. Not a trap.

**What pulls them upstairs:** gold-dusted pepper on the stairs, and the second-story office door. The fancy crate sits on the way, so they see it without a scavenger hunt. No lamp on — power's off.

### The grinding: hamster crane (no fight)

It's a **treadwheel crane**. Giant space hamster still walking because nobody stopped it. The crane arm is what they heard going up.

**Why it's here:** it's the engine for the crane that lifts sacks to the loft. Cheaper than paying a guy. Doesn't steal pepper. Power's off, so nothing electric is moving — the hamster is still walking because nobody took it out of the wheel. Varrow left. The hamster didn't. Feed sacks in the bulk pile are its dinner.

If they ask at the table:

> There's a giant hamster in a metal wheel. The wheel turns the crane. That's how they get the sacks upstairs. Power's off. The hamster doesn't know.

Feed it leftover pie (Animal Handling DC 10, or no roll) or jam the wheel (Athletics / thieves' tools DC 13). Then the stairs.

### The floor: bulk vs the one crate

Most of the warehouse is **not worth taking**. Perception DC 10: it is heavy, it is cheap, and if they spent a week finding a buyer on Bral they might get **about 40 gp**. They launch tonight. Leave it.

If they're poking around for loot, read this:

> Sacks. A lot of sacks. Bulk pepper, the cheap kind, leaking gold dust at the seams. Iron-bound cinnamon logs you couldn't lift without a week and a cart. A bin of hamster feed. Cheap tin cups. A cracked millstone. A broom with no handle. Three empty pepper tins, one labeled "do not eat," one labeled "eat," one unlabeled and probably the same stuff. A coil of rope that's more knot than rope. A left boot. A right boot, different size. A ledger with nothing in it but tallies and a coffee ring. Under a tarp: more sacks.
>
> None of this is treasure. It's a spice warehouse somebody walked out of. If you spent a week finding a buyer on Bral you might get forty gold for the lot. You don't have a week.
>
> One crate isn't like the others. Smaller. Cedar. Brass corners. On the way to the stairs. A tag still tacked on, a few months old: HOLD — SPECIALTY — DO NOT LOAD WITH BULK.

**The crate that stands out** (on the way to the stairs): smaller, cedar, brass corners, a **manifest from a few months ago** still tacked on. Stamped `HOLD — SPECIALTY — DO NOT LOAD WITH BULK`. Investigation DC 12 on the tag: *Matched sending stones. Hold for V. Do not separate.* Dated a few months back. The latch is **booby-trapped** (different from the office): Perception or Investigation DC 14 to spot a needle; thieves' tools DC 14 to lift it. Failure: CON DC 13 poison, poisoned for 1 hour.

**Inside (Nolan):** one **Sending Stone**. Not sitting on top. Read this when they open it:

> The lid comes up on old cloves and wet paper. This was not packed to be found in a hurry. A cracked ceramic jar of what used to be saffron, now a brown smear. Three tarnished spice-scoops, a tangle of packing twine, a child's wooden top with the paint worn off. A folded empty canvas sack, the Varrow & Daughters mark faded almost to nothing. A rusted padlock with no key. Shreds of a Bral dock receipt, months old, the lot number matching the tag. Under that: crumpled invoices, a moth-eaten scarf, one shoe, a cork, a bent nail. It looks like a drawer someone meant to sort and never did.

**Finding the stone: Investigation DC 13.** Korran is +0 on Investigation; Lucy or Pip may be better, or he can Help. Success: at the bottom, wrapped in oilcloth, a smooth dark stone the size of a thumb, faintly warm, a pair-mark etched on one face — and no second stone. Failure: junk. They can dump the crate and retry with advantage, or take the whole box and try again at the Pie Parlor. Don't let them miss it forever if they keep the crate.

They cannot sell a singleton. If someone uses it: **if nobody is carrying the other stone, the user immediately knows, and Sending is not cast.** Where the other stone is is **undecided** (Nolan). Do not decide it when they try. That is a later hook.

### The office door: magical lock and trap

The door at the top of the stairs is closed. Silver filigree in the frame (**Arcane Lock**). A second, fainter rune on the threshold (**Glyph of Warding**).

**Spot the trap:** Investigation or Arcana DC 14. Lucy is the likely Arcana (+4). Korran can Help. On a success they see both runes before anyone touches the handle.

**The lock:** Arcane Lock on a cheap latch. Thieves' tools **DC 20** (Korran is about +7, so he needs a 13 — a real roll). *Knock* or *Dispel Magic* drops it to a normal DC 10 pick, if anyone has the spell. Bashing: Athletics DC 20, and the glyph fires.

**The glyph (Nolan):** Explosive Runes, **thunder**, 3rd-level Glyph of Warding. 20-foot sphere on the landing. DEX DC 14, **5d8 thunder** (half on a success). Windows rattle. The warehouse booms. A dog barks two buildings over.

They should think a dock patrol heard that. **Nobody did.** Do not send a watchman. Give them hurried energy for the desk (grab the photo, the card, the chart) and the exit. If they flee without the clues, the office is still there — they can still snatch and run. Do not tell them the street stayed empty.

*Dispel Magic* on the threshold, or smearing the rune (Arcana DC 15; failure triggers it), kills the glyph before the door opens.

Don't make the door a twenty-minute puzzle. One detect, one pick or dispel, one save if they rush, then the desk.

### What Korran is actually rolling

**The Split Current is inside the [Ral'taka Expanse](../locations/raltaka-expanse.md).** The Expanse itself is easy to find. The merchant route *within* it is what this chart is for. The Split Current is a fork in the warm gold stream, among the islands, on that inner route.

Put the two papers on the desk, side by side.

**Varrow's chart** (everyone can read this, **no roll**): an ordinary merchant map, all in **Common**. It shows how to reach a labeled place:

> the Split Current

That **is** the destination for tonight, pass or fail. Anyone at the table can read the name. If they take the chart, they have the inner route. The head already said *"Through the gold. Follow the warm current, until the islands."* Same heading.

**Korran's treasure map** is a different question. Two doodles near the hoard have never meant a place. The DC 13 (Intelligence with navigator's tools, Korran; Lucy Helps) is only: *is one of those doodles the same mark Varrow labeled?*

- **Success:** yes. The Split Current is also the first step toward the hoard. Chase Varrow or chase the gold: same first jump. The other doodle stays blank.
- **Failure:** they still go to the Split Current. They have the name, and they have Varrow's chart. What they do **not** get is the link to the treasure map. Do **not** retry at the helm tonight. Do **not** voice the navigator. Session 6, Matt can slap both maps down and argue. That is his entrance.

**They always leave with a heading.** Worst case is "we are following Varrow's chart to the Split Current" and Korran still doesn't know if that's on *his* map. That is enough to launch.

**They leave with:** the face, the torn card, Varrow's chart, a heading, and maybe one Sending Stone. Then the parlor. Hurry out of the warehouse, because they think the thunder was heard. Nobody is coming. They can sleep.
