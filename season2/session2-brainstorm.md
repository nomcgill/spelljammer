## Should the Stromboli case be why they leave Bral?

**My recommendation: yes, but indirectly.** Solve the case on Bral, so it stays the small mystery you want, with a real answer and a real culprit. But **the answer is what forces them off-planet.**

The person behind the theft has **already left for the Ral'taka Expanse**, and what they're after there threatens every PC's motive. So the party wins the Bral mystery and immediately learns it was the first move in something bigger.

Each PC gets a reason to follow:
- **Lucy:** recovering the card closes Marfa's case, but the ingredient and its source are still out there.
- **Korran:** it's his homeland, and possibly his past.
- **Pip:** Marfa's backing depends on the supply line.
- **Megan:** his debt to Korran, plus Marfa's promise to get him home afterwards.

## The Kenku guild's name

Proposed names for the Kenku assassins hired through the Annex:

1. **The Unkindness.** An "unkindness" is the word for a flock of ravens. It's quiet and slightly funny, and it says what they do. **My pick.**
2. **The Murmuration**, after a flock of starlings moving as one. It's elegant, and it suits a crew whose members are hard to tell apart.
3. **The Last Word.** Kenku can only repeat what they've heard, so their victims literally get the last word. It also suits the self-disintegrating rings.
4. **The Hush.** It's simple and menacing, and easy to say at the table.

**Their signature:** each contractor carries a ruby Ring of Disintegrate, so a failed job leaves no body and no witness. That's the Annex's *"Discretion"* made literal. *Proposed:* the rings are guild property. **A ring that isn't returned is a debt**, so whoever holds the dead Kenku's ring has the guild's attention.

## The Case of the Missing Stromboli (proposed answer)

### What was stolen

On Bugsy's reopening night, Babe made his legendary Stromboli at **the corner table**, then gave the leather folder holding his handwritten **recipe card** to the staff to lock away. **By closing, the card was gone.**

The card holds two secrets:
- **The secret ingredient:** *goldfleck pepper*, which grows only on the gold-veined floating islands of the Ral'taka Expanse.
- **The grower's mark,** written in Babe's hand at the bottom. It's a sign the Hadozee growers recognise, and without it no ship is welcome at the groves. Only Babe ever wrote it down. The client **tears this strip off** and takes it to the Expanse.

### How it was stolen

**A Kenku from the same guild lifted it** by mimicking **Bugsy's voice**. A server heard "Bugsy" ask for the card, slipped it out of the folder and handed it over, while the real Bugsy was in the kitchen. The folder was left empty. Marfa has quietly suspected her own daughter ever since. That's family tension for Lucy, and a clue the party can crack.

### Who hired it

*Proposed:* a former quartermaster of a pirate crew that **sailed in partnership with the *Star-Reaver***. They now run a respectable-looking **spice import house in Bral's docks**. Placeholder name: *Varrow & Daughters, Fine Spices*.
- **What they want:** they've wanted back into the Ral'taka Expanse for the gold, and the hoard, ever since the *Star-Reaver* vanished.
- **How they got the route:** they bought it from the **Psurlons**. The Ringer that wore Krysta knew the route from her memories.
- **What was missing:** the **grower's mark**, which only Babe's card has. So they hired the theft through the Annex.

Because this ties the case to Korran's past, **check it with Brad.**

**Alternative clients** if that's too much Korran too soon:
- **The plasmoid court**, wanting the Expanse's gold for Pip's brother.
- **Tino Fennetti**, selling the family out again to someone else.

### Why the Kenku attacked now

The client heard that **the head had arrived on Bral**, because the Annex reads the dock record that Lawrence took. They also heard that Marfa was recruiting outsiders. The head is the one thing that could name the client, so they hired a second contract: **the head first, Marfa if needed.**

### Nicole's twist: "What if the recipe was never meant to be stolen?"

It wasn't about the recipe. **It was about the grower's mark**, the key to a door into Korran's homeland that the Ruths have held for years without knowing it mattered.

### How the case resolves on Bral

The party traces the theft to the spice house, fights there, and **recovers the card, with the bottom torn off** (Nolan's choice). The recipe is all there, but the strip with the grower's mark in Babe's handwriting is gone. The client's ship has **already launched** for the Expanse with that strip. Case closed, client gone, and every PC now has a reason to follow.

## What's in Krysta's head

The head holds the real Krysta's memories, plus the Ringer's own. If the party gets it questioned, the rewards are:

1. **The client's face and name.** The Ringer sold the route to them in person. This confirms the culprit, or gives a lead if the party is stuck.
2. **The safe route into the Ral'taka Expanse.** Krysta was the Ruths' courier for the pepper shipments and knew the way in. **Korran's map gives the route *within* the Expanse** to the hoard. **The head gives the way *to* it.** Together, they get the party there.
3. **For Megan, news from home.** Krysta was taken *after* Megan was petrified, so she knows how the **war between the Red and Brown Lions** stood when she left. That's the one thing Megan most wants to know.
4. **What the IOU was for.** The Pie Parlors IOU on her body was the Ruths' payment for a pepper shipment. That answers Megan's question about the IOU, and explains why Krysta was at the Psurlon hub at all.
5. **How Krysta died.** This is Nolan's hook from the hook bank. It's personal for Megan, and how much to show is your call.

**How to get it questioned:** Megan doesn't know Speak with Dead. *Proposed:* a dockside funeral chapel for spacefarers, **The Last Berth**, whose priest launches the dead into the Astral Sea and can cast Speak with Dead for a fee. Marfa knows the priest.

### The poison

*Proposed:* the dart carried a **memory-rot**, the guild's speciality, made to erase what a target knows. On a dead head it works slowly. **Every few hours the head loses something.** That puts a clock on Session 2 without anyone chasing the party.

## Megan's reaction to the dart

A proposed beat, in two halves:

1. **Indifference first.** He pulls the dart out and shrugs: *"It is not my daughter. It ate my daughter."* That fits how casually he's been carrying it.
2. **Then the turn.** Someone, perhaps Marfa or a PC, points out that the head remembers things from **after** Megan was turned to stone, including the war. Megan goes very still. **This head is the only news he has of home**, and the poison is eating it. Now he's the most urgent person at the table.

His deal with Marfa follows from that. He wants the head questioned before the rot finishes, and passage home after. In return, he lends his sword to whatever the Ruths need. Marfa, of course, already knows what she needs.

## How Session 2 can unfold

### The opening

Marfa is flustered, but practised. *"This happens. Not often, but it happens."* Her first move is always the Iron Annex: find out who hired the guild, then **outpay them or lean on them**. She makes her deals with each PC, which the dart interrupted in Session 1, and sends them out.

### Ways into the mystery

The party can take these in any order. Each gives a piece of the answer.

| Avenue | What happens | What they learn |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Iron Annex: outpay** | Tilptek explains the counter-contract policy: beat the original fee and the contract is voided. Lucy has the Ruth name, Pip has diamonds | The contract's price, and that **the target was the head**. Not the client's name, because that's "Discretion" |
| **The Iron Annex: pressure** | Lean on Tilptek, threaten the Annex's reputation, or go over his head to "the boss" | Very dangerous. Could get the client's contract alias, or a meeting with the guild |
| **The ruby ring** | Show it, or the guild comes for it | The ring is guild property. Returning it buys a meeting with the guild, and a guild member will name who hired the job, for a price |
| **The reopening night** | Question the parlor staff and Bugsy | **"Bugsy" asked for the card, but Bugsy was in the kitchen.** That's Kenku mimicry, which links the theft to the assassin and clears Bugsy |
| **The Joisey Jiffs** | A soft-G showdown at Astro Nommie's Slicehouse | They're innocent, but someone offered to **sell** them the recipe. They refused out of pride. The seller left a spice-house trade card, or smelled of goldfleck |
| **Lawrence and the docks** | Ask who's been buying dock records | The Annex buys them daily. A spice importer's ship recently filed for departure |
| **The DAV** | Korran registers and names the Shrike | Spots the spice house's ship, and its flight plan toward the Expanse |
| **The Last Berth** | Get the head questioned before the rot finishes | The client's face, the route into the Expanse, and news of Megan's war |

**The convergence:** any two or three of these point at **the spice house**. The Annex and the guild give the name, the Jiffs and the docks give the place, and the DAV gives the deadline.

### The climax

Raid the spice house, recover the card, and learn the ship has gone. Then the party heads for the Shrike.

## Combat at the Iron Annex

**Fixed:** there's a fight when the party visits. **Proposed:** some reasons why, which can be combined:

1. **They walk in on the reissue.** This is my recommendation.
   - **The setup:** a failed contract gets reissued, and when the party arrives, **a fresh team from the Kenku guild is at the desk** collecting the new contract on the head. Megan has just carried their target through the front door.
   - **The trigger:** Tilptek, with perfect politeness, tells the Kenku their target has entered the lobby. *"Efficiency."*
   - **Why it works:** it follows directly from Session 1, needs no new villain, and puts the guild face to face with the party.
2. **The ring sets off the Annex's wards.** The rings are **Annex-issued property**, tagged and tracked. Carrying a dead contractor's ring through the door reads as "contractor killed, asset stolen," and Thri-kreen compliance staff respond. This only works if the party took the ring. If Marfa has it, she carries it in.
3. **Pressure goes wrong.** If the party chooses "pressure" over paying, the Annex defends itself.
   - Thri-kreen staff **drop their camouflage** off the walls, and the players realise there were far more of them than they'd counted.
   - It's the natural consequence of a choice the players made, so it feels earned.
4. **The boss's audition.** The boss watches on a monitor and sends Thri-kreen enforcers to **test** the party. Afterwards Tilptek says, *"Results. Results. The boss would like to offer you a contract."* That sets the Annex up as a future patron, and turns Season 1's manhunt around.
5. **The client is in the building.** The client's agent has come in to raise the fee after the failure. They see the party and bolt, and their bodyguards hold the door. This turns into a chase, and it shows the players the client's people early.

**My suggestion:** use 1 as the trigger, then let the Thri-kreen **stay neutral**, until the party breaks something.
- **The neutrality:** *"The lobby is a neutral zone. Damages will be invoiced."*
- **If something breaks:** the staff join the fight, and that's when 3 or 4 kicks in.

### The Annex as a battlefield

All from the [Annex's established description](../locations/iron-annex.md#atmosphere), plus *proposed* touches:

- **The pneumatic doors** hiss open with their *chhk-thoom*. They're good for slamming on someone, or getting stuck halfway.
- **The sigils in the floor** shift. *Proposed:* they're wards, and they flare under anyone who draws a weapon.
- **The light panels** strobe or cut out. Kenku in the dark, **mimicking the party's voices**, are the scariest version of this fight.
- **The holographic motto sign** can be smashed, flicker or glitch: *"Results. Results. Results. Results."*
- **Message tubes** send contracts whooshing overhead.
- **The cubicles** are a maze of Thri-kreen clerks. *Proposed:* Thri-kreen don't sleep in 5e, and these clerks **keep working through the fight**, typing on with two arms while ducking with the other two.
- **The Thri-kreen** coordinate by **telepathy**, so the staff move silently and all at once.

## Why Scritch has blindsight

Kenku don't normally have blindsight, so it needs a reason. Nolan's open question. None of these is canon yet.

1. **He listens his way through the dark (echolocation).** Kenku can reproduce any sound perfectly, so a few of them train to click and hear the echo come back, like a bat. It's the same gift the guild already uses for mimicry, just turned into a weapon. **At the table:** a soft, fast *tk-tk-tk* in the dark just before he strikes. A Silence spell, or loud enough noise (the brass tubes, the glitching motto sign), would throw him off.
2. **He's blind.** His eyes are milky white. The guild gave the one blind Kenku the Darkness ring because it costs him nothing: the dark is home ground for him. **At the table:** when the lights come back, the party sees his eyes and realizes he never needed them. This goes well with the first option, since being blind is the reason he learned to echolocate.
3. **The guild matches each ring to its Kenku.** The Unkindness gives out rings deliberately, so each spell fits a skill the Kenku already has. Scritch gets Darkness because he can fight in it. This makes the guild seem organized and frightening, and tells the players the next Kenku will come with a different combination.
4. **Annex hardware.** Director Tallyworth fitted him with an autognome-made device, such as a brass ear-cone or a vibration-sensing implant, because the Annex has paid for this guild's gear before. **At the table:** a faint mechanical whir. It ties the Annex closer to the killers than it admits, and it gives the party something they can break.
5. **A pact.** He bargained with something that lives in the dark. This is the eeriest option, but it raises questions that the session doesn't need.

**My pick:** combine 1 and 2. He's blind and echolocates. It needs no new lore beyond what Kenku already do, it gives him something to show at the table and a weakness the players can find (noise), and the reveal of his eyes is a good moment when the lights come back. Option 3 works with it as background.
