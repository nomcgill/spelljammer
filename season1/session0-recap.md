# Season 1 — Session 0 Recap

Read this aloud the week before Season 2 begins. Player-facing; no DM secrets.

---

You still remember the day you arrived at Warden's Maw.

Four strangers. One cafeteria meal time. A prison carved into an oblong asteroid that had drifted out of Wildspace twenty years ago — past every law, every government, every watching eye — and into the hands of Githyanki who ran it for profit. Most who entered expected to die there. Mining coal. Mining diamond dust. Waiting to be useful to someone, somewhere, someday.

You did not die there... because one night, the sky tore open.

Plasmoid ships arrived to bombard the prison — not for you, not for mercy, but to free their vice president. Gu. The soft-spoken inmate who had been warning you for a thousand meals to stay away from the windows. Who nobody believed. Who had been right.

They freed him. And Gu, instead of vanishing into the arms of his fleet, came for you. Cell by cell. One by one. _"Follow me!"_

He led you onto a balcony and screamed his freedom into the void — _"I'm free! I'm down here! Here!"_ — and then a Githyanki ship obliterated him for the trouble.

In the chaos that followed, Luckyluciano's nephew, Jetson, pulled up in the chaos in a borrowed Flying Fish called the _DiceGunner_ (which was a family ship), crashed it, and you fought your way with Jetson to a stolen Githyanki Damselfly while a battleship charged its cannon at the dock behind you. One guard fell to your blades. The other — Aryndac — was tackled into the Astral Sea by Gu, who had actually survived the earlier blast. But unnoticed by you, Aryndac Misty Stepped onto your ship mid-fall, and vanished into the walls.

Onboard the Githyanki Damselfly, you punched it into hyperspeed.

For a moment, it almost felt like escape. Three million miles an hour. The Rock of Bral one or two days out. A.R.I.A. (**A**stral-**R**esponsive **I**ntelligence **A**ssistant) the Damselfly's elegant, faintly sassy intelligence, informing you that this was not, in fact, the fastest ship in the fleet — and also that a Githyanki was already aboard.

You found Aryndac at the hyperspace drive, his longsword driven into the heart of the machine. He smirked. He teleported away. He left you with a crippled drive that would need diamonds to repair and a poison in Jetson's abdomen that would kill him in forty-eight hours.

Spelljammer ships automatically exit hyperspeed when they encounter a planet or entity of note. As such, it wasn't long before the ship lurched to a halt beside a ghost: a squid ship with broken tentacles and a hull built from the ribcage of a dead god. Korran knew her: The _Star-Reaver_. The pirate vessel that had plundered his homeland, enslaved him, and eventually had become his home for a time. Investigating the ship's wreckage, with a tainted, acidic green aura, Korran found a Fish Suit and a pair of Boots of Gravitation. The rest you left behind as the ship's obvious ailment risked the clean air of your own ship's air envelope.

Something followed you into the next jump. A Feyr—a tentacled horror that feeds quietly on strong emotions. To minimize the chance of harm to itself, it prefers to devour the nightmares of other creatures while they sleep. Luckyluciano burned half your rations trying to kill it.

And then Tamenik began to glow — black veins under his shell, a fever that was not a fever. Blackstar Fever. A curse. He slipped into unconsciousness, and into a dream where an old Hadozee captain sat at a spelljamming helm and told him two things: that he had a week to live without a spell of Greater Restoration or voidfish eggs… and also that Korran's treasure map was encrypted by the cipher of Korran's late mother's birthday. The captain had loved her. His name was Gloamcast. He was the one whose corpse had sat at the helm of the _Star-Reaver_.

To find solutions for your two sick shipmates, you went looking for a nearby Psurlon civilization in the hollowed remains of a dead god. A.R.I.A. gave you three coordinates. The first was not a dead god.

It was a Cosmic Horror.

It tore a wing from your ship. You climbed onto the roof and worked the cannons while the hyperspace drive spun — thirty seconds that felt like forever. Mid-fight, when the ship was about to take massive damage,time itself stopped for everyone but Pip. A Chwinga, freed from a Feyr's bronze eye, giggled. Called him a funny color. Offered to help the big thing attack the ship. It left him the Charm of Ruin, and vanished to the void.

You escaped. On your next jump, you found the real hub: a Psurlon shipping yard in the ribs of a god. You needed diamond for the drive, voidfish eggs for Tamenik, and Astral silk for Jetson. The Psurlons wanted favours — especially anything stamped with Luckyluciano Ruth's family name.

While Krysta — who claimed to be a Leonin king's daughter — showed the others around the yard, Korran slipped off among the containers searching for your goods, or anything valuable. In one of them he found a Leonin frozen in stone like cargo. A Scroll of Greater Restoration was stuck to the container with green goo. Instead of saving the scroll to possibly cure Tamenik, Korran used it and freed the Leonin, who proudly proclaimed himself Megan Forthfang, King of the Red Lions.

Pip, in a moment of distraction, swallowed a fortune in diamond he'd found— and noticed, in the same heartbeat, that someone somewhere was scrying him. Krysta tried to turn you against Megan. She was no daughter: she was a Ringer, an impostor Psurlon who had eaten the real Krysta and worn her memories like a coat. You ran. You fought. You left with Megan, a hundred thousand gold pieces worth of diamond dust in Pip the plasmoid's stomach, and a severed head of a Psurlon, shaped as a lion's head, that still knows things it shouldn't.

Megan cured Tamenik with Greater Restoration. He cured Jetson's poison later after he rested. He had never been on a spaceship before and he now owes Korran a debt.

A large portion of the diamonds fed and repaired the damaged hyperspace drive. Bral was one day away.

A.R.I.A. warned of something enormous closing from behind — a familiar ship. The same mothership that had bombarded Warden's Maw. A wave of Dispel Magic hit you like an EMP. Auxiliary power. Emergency lights. A.R.I.A. gone silent. On the flickering monitor: Alkgleb, a plasmoid general serving Pip's brother the king… and Kirrak, Tamenik's own nephew, the CEO who had locked him away for being annoying with his drums.

They had scried Pip to find you. Tamenik cannot be scried. That is still true.

_"Send in the other ones,"_ Alkgleb said in Primordial.

A silent silver assassin slipped aboard the darkened Damselfly with a silver sword and summoned a demon.

Korran was already gone. He had slipped out into the vacuum — three hundred feet of open space, on an incredible stealth roll — and found a crack in the hull of the gargantuan mothership. He squeezed inside, meaning to cripple the ship itself, or to reach Alkgleb and Kirrak. What he found instead, in the mothership's loading bay, was a smaller ship with a spelljamming helm waiting to be used.

Back aboard the Damselfly, the rest of you fought. You killed the demon. You killed the assassin. And that bought exactly enough time.

Marfa Ruth — Luckyluciano's mother — arrived in the _Slice Runner_ at the last possible moment, with a threat that could end plasmoid business with Babe Ruth's Pie Parlors forever, and beamed you off your crippled ship. Everyone. Jetson. Megan, holding the lion-shaped severed head that had the memories of his daughter. All of you but one.

Because Korran had taken the helm of that smaller ship, hijacked it out of the mothership's own loading bay, and flown it out into the dark.

Your two ships crossed the last stretch of the void side by side — the _Slice Runner_, carrying the crew and Marfa, and a stolen plasmoid vessel with a Hadozee at the helm — and together you all reached the Rock of Bral.

Everyone made it back.

And Pip, standing on solid ground for the first time since the Maw, already had a plan: gather allies, and lead a revolution against his usurping brother, the king.

The Next Jump continues.
