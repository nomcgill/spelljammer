# House Rules

Deliberate departures from [Spelljammer: Adventures in Space](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/sais) and other published material. Everything here is canon for "The Next Jump" and overrides the book.

Reconstructed from what actually happened in Season 1. Where a number was never stated at the table, it's marked `[unconfirmed]` rather than invented.

## Hyperspeed and the hyperspace drive

The party's Githyanki [Damselfly](ships/the-damselfly.md) has a **hyperspace drive** with a **Hyperdrive Core**, which is not how spelljamming works in the book.

| Rule | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Top speed | **3 million miles per hour** ("3 mmph") |
| Warden's Maw to the Rock of Bral at top speed | 1–2 days |
| Spin-up time before launch | **5 turns / 30 seconds** |
| Launch command | "PUNCH IT" |
| Fuel | **Diamond or diamond dust**, consumed by the Hyperdrive Core |
| Auto-exit | Ships **automatically exit hyperspeed** when they encounter a planet or entity of note |

**Damage and degradation.** A blade driven into the drive cripples it: maximum speed drops and piloting becomes much harder. Restoring it requires diamond. Feeding it 100,000 gp of diamond dust brought a multi-day trip back to one day.

**Without a working drive**, per the adventure ideas doc, a one-day journey becomes **445 years**. Time is treated loosely out here, which is the joke and also the threat.

**Being dropped from hyperspeed** is something a larger ship can do to you — the plasmoid mothership did it with a wave of Dispel Magic that functioned as an EMP.

## Ship combat and systems

- **Ship cannons** deal **8d8 force damage** per blast. The Damselfly has three on the roof and more on the underside.
- **Damselfly wings** are only used for flying through atmosphere. Losing one doesn't impair travel in the Astral Sea (established on a History check, DC 5).
- **A Dispel Magic wave** can act as a ship-scale EMP: auxiliary power only, ship intelligence offline, comms and monitors barely functional, emergency lighting.
- **Air envelopes** can overlap and contaminate. A derelict with a larger, toxic envelope will progressively poison a smaller one parked inside it, and the toxicity distributes evenly — so anti-hazard precautions and sealing don't help.
- **Ships can be scried.** The Githyanki can probably scry their own stolen Damselfly, which is why stopping is dangerous.
- **Sending comms** exist ship-to-ship across the void.
- **Teleportation circles** can be installed aboard ships and used to extract people from other ships — unless something aboard the target blocks it.

## Blackstar Fever

A homebrew affliction. **It is a curse, not a disease or a poison** — stated explicitly by its previous carrier, which is why ordinary disease cures don't apply.

- **Symptoms:** sudden black veins beneath the skin, glowing faintly. Nightmares. Unconsciousness.
- **Mechanical effect:** disadvantage on Wisdom and Intelligence checks and saves.
- **Time to death:** less than a week.
- **Cures that work:** Greater Restoration, **voidfish eggs** swallowed whole, or a unicorn horn.
- **What cured Tamenik:** [Megan Forthfang](npcs/megan-forthfang.md) — a **high-level paladin** — cast **Greater Restoration**. The Chwinga and Charm of Ruin were not involved; the unicorn horn went unused. This is canon.
- **Transmission:** passes from carrier to carrier. The previous carrier was [Captain Gloamcast](npcs/gloamcast.md) of the *Star-Reaver*, who died of it and assumed it would die with him. `[unconfirmed]` how it actually transmits, and whether curing it ends the chain or passes it on.

## Githyanki blade poison

The poison on [Aryndac](npcs/aryndac.md)'s longsword, which he used on both the hyperspace drive and [Jetson](npcs/jetson.md).

- Settles **deep in the abdomen** and attacks the victim's brain, specifically their "synaptic transmissions."
- **Symptoms:** hallucination. "Space has never looked so gray before…"
- **48 hours to live** untreated.
- Also described as a **psychic curse**, which is why the intended treatment was **astral silk tightened around the wound** rather than an antitoxin. Greater Restoration also works.
- Side effect: while Jetson was poisoned, **the ship's maximum speed was reduced** — his condition and the ship's performance were linked.

## Anti-scrying and anti-teleportation

[Tamenik](player-characters/tamenik-freelin.md) cannot be scried or teleported. The notes attribute this to something about **his shell**, and Megan suspects a curse.

Two consequences established in play:

1. Hunters who want him must scry **someone near him** instead and follow that person.
2. His presence aboard a ship **blocks teleportation extraction from that ship** — including a teleportation circle operated from outside.

`[unconfirmed]` the radius, whether it blocks teleportation *by* the party as well as *at* them, and whether Greater Restoration or Remove Curse ends it.

## Creature reskins

Nolan reskins published stat blocks freely rather than using the creature as written. Recorded in [`bestiary/season1-encounters.md`](bestiary/season1-encounters.md):

- **Megan Forthfang**, a Leonin king, uses **Xenk Yendar**'s stat block (CR 10) but is "as strong as a level 17 Paladin," and casts spells Xenk doesn't have.
- **The silver plasmoid** uses **Drow Inquisitor (Summoner Variant)** (CR 14).
- **A Feyr's bronze eyes** can imprison a Chwinga's essence — not a published property.

The rule of thumb: the stat block is a starting point for numbers, and the fiction overrides it.
